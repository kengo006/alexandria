## Critique

### Pre-committed standards (written before reading the draft)

For this opening to hold, it must:

- **X** — mark the difference between "the memorial shapes how the memory is held" (weak, nearly uncontested) and "the memorial is a condition of the memory existing" (strong, contested). An argument that slides between them wins nothing.
- **Y** — meet the obvious objection that people had to want the monument first, so something preceded it.
- **Z** — say what would count as evidence either way, given that both readings predict the same monuments.

**Most likely fatal flaw**: the strong claim will be asserted and the weak one defended.

### Overall judgment

- Main claim: public memory is produced through commemorative practice rather than stored by it.
- Strength: **moderate**. X is met once and then lost; Y is not engaged; Z is absent.

### Specific criticisms

**1 — The two readings collapse in §1 ¶3**
- Location: paragraph 3, "and so the monument makes the memory possible."
- Problem: paragraphs 1–2 argue the weak reading (commemoration shapes what is remembered) and paragraph 3 concludes the strong one. The supporting quotation from Doe (2011) p. 47 is the strong reading; the argument leading to it is not.
- Repair direction: either carry the strong reading from the start and pay for it, or state the conclusion in the weak form the argument actually earned.

**2 — The strongest objection is present in the vault and unanswered**
- Location: the whole section.
- Problem: Roe (1998) p. 12 argues that a monument is downstream of a constituency that already exists. The draft does not mention it. An examiner who knows the literature will supply it in the first minute.
- Repair direction: engage it directly. The available move is that Roe's "wanting" is itself formed by earlier commemorative practice — but that has to be argued, not assumed.

**3 — Overclaiming vocabulary**
- Location: paragraph 4, "obviously, no memory of this kind survives without its monuments."
- Problem: "obviously" is carrying the argument. And "no memory of this kind survives without" is a stronger claim than anything cited supports.
- Repair direction: drop the adverb; narrow to the cases actually in evidence.

### No criticism

- Paragraphs 1–2 as an account of the weak reading: the reasoning is clean and the Ives (2014) distinction is used correctly.
- The framing sentence that opens the chapter does its job and does not need work.
