# Changelog

The skeleton is extracted from a live production system; each release is a **curated snapshot**, not a mirror. Releases are named after the upstream snapshot they carry — the system was already in its third generation when first published, hence the repo's first release was tagged v0.1 and later releases adopt the upstream `v3.x` numbering.

## v3.9 — 2026-08-23 · What the second reading found

v3.8 was read once — while it was being written. Read again three hours later, its central new section had **one argument that was simply wrong** and three sentences that claimed more than the evidence. 🔑 **The mechanical audit found none of them, and could not have**: every one of them is internally coherent. A scan looks for two places that disagree; these were single sentences that agreed with themselves and were false.

- 🔴🔴 **The reason given for not repairing a damaged word was the wrong reason.** v3.8 said: do not correct `inferiority` to `interiority` before quoting, because "the reader would be given a sentence that is in no edition of the book". **That is backwards.** The repaired sentence *is* the author's wording and *is* in every properly set edition. What it is not is *on the page being cited*. ⇒ the section now states the real shape, which is sharper than the mistake it replaces: **quote it as printed and the words are not the author's; repair it and the words are not on the page. Either way the pairing of quotation and page credential is false** — and that pairing is the whole product.
- 🔴 **"A quotation that does not exist in the work"** — it does exist, in that copy, printed on that page. That is precisely what makes it dangerous. Now: *a string that is not the author's text*, with the distinction spelled out rather than blurred.
- 🔴 **"The only defence available is a list"** — false. Replacing the copy is a defence, and a better one; it is simply a procurement problem rather than one the pipeline can solve. Now scoped: *the defence, for as long as this is the only copy you have*.
- 🔴 **"The three routinely disagree, which is the entire reason the credential is worth anything"** — contradicted by the three reasons listed four lines above it. Now: *which is why a bare "p. 47" says nothing until you state which of them you mean*.
- **`examples/README.md`: a dangling referent introduced by v3.8's own fix.** Moving the fabrication warning to the top left the section below opening with "This matters more here than in most repositories" — where "this" now pointed at a parenthetical about warning placement. **A repair that creates a smaller version of the defect it repaired.**
- **`dead_link_scan.py --self-test`.** The previous release shipped a scanner whose discriminating power rested on the author having fault-injected it once, in private. It now carries four fixtures and asserts the verdict on each, so the claim is checkable by whoever is holding it rather than taken on trust. ⚠ Its range is printed with its result: **it proves separation on the four cases it was shown, and says nothing about link forms nobody thought to write down.** 📌 A note from building it: the first positive control "passed" because the broken build crashed — **a crash and a detection both exit 1**, and telling them apart needs the output, not the status code.
- **`sync-matrix` gains the credential-floor row.** The upstream rule and the published §1 are separately worded, not one copied from the other — which is exactly the condition the matrix exists to track. It was added the day the second wording appeared, rather than the day it drifted.

## v3.8 — 2026-08-23 · The ground truth that can be wrong

Three items the previous release listed as *not done* and one word that had never been defined. The largest is a limit on this repository's oldest rule: the rendered-page credential assumes the page is ground truth, and there is a class of source where it is not.

- 🔴 **`claims-and-evidence` §1 gains a floor: the page can be wrong, and then the credential confirms the error.** One upstream work is a *reflowed typesetting* of a scan — **image count 0**, so the damaged words are real glyphs, set as type, with nothing left to re-OCR and no rendering setting that helps. A page prints *"the deprivatized province of **inferiority**"* where the argument requires *interiority*, and two lines above prints *"interior domain"*: **the page contradicts itself.** 🔑 **The defect is a real English word**, so every layer passes — correspondence, not-second-hand, entity attribution, folio present, sentence genuinely on that page ⇒ **a ✅ with a full credential, on a quotation that exists in no edition of the book.** The defence is a register (`verbatim_unsafe`) consulted *before* the ✅ is issued; location and pages stay usable, verbatim does not, and ⚠ the damaged form is **never** quietly repaired before quoting — that manufactures a source. **The shape outlives the volume: a check whose ground truth can itself be defective needs a register of the cases where it is, because by construction the check cannot find them.**
- 🔴 **`dead_link_scan.py` had never looked at this repository.** It only scanned a vault's wikilinks, and on a checkout with no vault it printed `notes root not found` and exited with a failure status — which reads as *your links are broken* and means *I had nothing to look at*. Meanwhile the repo's own 23 document links had been checked by nothing, for seven releases. It now runs two passes: **document links** (`[text](path)` across the repo, always) and **wikilinks** (when a notes root exists), with each pass reporting its denominator and **"not run" printed differently from "ran and found nothing"** — the empty-sample rule `health_check.py` already applied to itself. Fault-injected before shipping. ⚠ **And its first run reported this changelog**, for the sentence explaining what a document link looks like: markdown inside a code span is documentation *about* links, not a link. 🔑 **A scanner for a documentation tree has to know the difference between the tree's subject and the tree's content** — code spans and fences are now stripped before matching, with the false positive and the true one both re-tested afterwards.
- 🔴 **`folio` was used twelve times across the rules, the digests and the examples, and defined nowhere.** It now has one definition, in §1 where the credential lives: *the number printed on the leaf*, as opposed to the PDF viewer's physical page index or a page block's position in an extracted text layer. ⚠ It was found by auditing the previous release's own additions — **not by any scan**, because a word that is used consistently and never defined is invisible to every check that looks for inconsistency.
- **`examples/README.md` now carries its fabrication warning above the table, not below it.** A reader who arrives, scans the table of three worked outputs and clicks through had already passed the section explaining that none of it is real.

## v3.6 — 2026-08-23 · The copies you did not touch

**A rule was reversed upstream, and its copies were not.** v3.5 was about the region a gate cannot see; this release is about the particular blind spot that opens when a rule is *replaced by its opposite* — the wrong text is neither added nor deleted, so both of the verification habits that catch everything else walk straight past it. It starts with the reversal that made the point, and this is the first release in which the thing found stating something untrue was **this repository's own spec**.

- 🔴🔴 **The Writer now reads the source, and this repo said otherwise in fourteen places.** Upstream, on 2026-08-19, the rule *"the Writer may never read source PDFs; the Searcher is its only window"* was reversed: **every quote and page number destined for the finished text is re-checked by the Writer at the source page.** The retrieval route is unchanged — what was added is a confirmation step at the shipping boundary. ⚠ **The reason is not distrust.** The spot-check that prompted it found *nothing wrong*: eleven retrieved items re-examined at the page, verbatim text, printed page number, running head and italics all matching. What it did find was five things nobody had asked for, and they changed the argument. 🔑 **A retrieval answers the question it was given; reading the page shows you what the page contains** — the gap is *granularity*, not accuracy: the question's grain became the reading's grain, and the literature does not know what your question looked like. 🔴 **Its range ships with it**: this buys the completeness of the page you were pointed at, and **nothing** about the page you were not — it cures an answer that was too narrow, never a search that looked in the wrong place, and it must not become the reason the retrieval layer relaxes. **The criterion is destination, not phase** — phase wording manufactures the escape hatch *"it was still exploratory then"*, and exploratory finds do reach final prose. **Cost**: one page render per quote, so batch it at the draft boundary. Landed in `roles/writer.md` §1, `roles/searcher.md`'s header, `governance/role-division.md` (role table *and* the read/write boundary), `governance/system-overview.md`, the README role table, `integration/agents/searcher.md`, the Writer wrapper's `description:` **and** its digest, `shared/summon-templates.md`, and `shared/council-mode.md`.
- 🔴 **New `sync-matrix` §4 — reversing a rule, including what this repo got wrong while doing it.** Two habits are structurally blind here: *check the new text is present* (it is — pass) and *check the diff's minus side for losses* (there are none — pass). **What is wrong was neither added nor removed; it is the old copy you never touched**, and both habits are built around the passage you edited. ⇒ a third direction, mandatory on reversal: **verify the abolished wording is gone**, across a domain with three dimensions — the pattern's boundary, **the path range** (agent definition files load into a subagent's system prompt on every spawn and are the easiest thing to leave out of a path list), and **the number of phrasings**. Every hit is then classified by a person into *still asserting* / *quoting it as abolished* / *history*, with the positive control — did the new wording actually land — run in the same pass. 🎫 **And the section reports its own failure**, because that is the useful part: the enumerated multi-family scan found **8** places; reading every one of the 110 lines in the tree that mention the object at all found **14**. The six misses came in three shapes — a prohibition with **no verb in it** (`source PDFs never`), the **rationale paragraph** that explains a rule nobody thought to re-read, and 🔴 **a table cell under a column headed "Never"**, in the two most-read files, whose text is the *affirmative sentence of the new rule*. **The negation was not in the sentence; it was in the column header.** Worse is the mirror: a positive control phrased as *"does the tree now say the Writer reads the source?"* would have counted those two cells as evidence the new rule was already in place. ⇒ **A scanner makes a large space searchable; it is not entitled to declare the space empty.** Shrink the denominator until a person can read all of it, run the scan inside it, and let the person adjudicate.
- 🔴 **`health_check.py` gains a fourth check, because the change record itself can stop.** Version numbers can be mirrored correctly into every location the matrix names while a file's own changelog quietly stops being written: the last entry ages, nothing disagrees with anything, and every mirror check stays green. Measured upstream while preparing this release — **of seven canonical rule files, four carried a header version that appears nowhere in their own changelog: eighteen rule changes with no record, the worst file thirteen of them.** ⚠ *That figure was written as nineteen first, by subtracting the changelog's newest version number from the header's; enumerated, it is eighteen, because one number in the span was never issued. It is corrected here rather than quietly — **a version number is not a version, and the distance between two numbers is not a count of the things between them**, which is the substitution this release is otherwise about.* 🔑 **The gate verified that the number was copied, not that the change was written down.** The new check is optional-by-config like the version mirrors, and **fault-injected before shipping** (a fixture whose log carries the current version passes; one whose log does not raises a warning). ⚠ Its range is stated in the file: it finds a header version missing from the log, **not a gap in the middle of one**. And the most-affected file upstream was the most actively edited — the record stops where the work is heaviest.
- **`shared/degradation-registry.md` is no longer one list.** A registry begins as *"the files whose grep lies"* and then the corpus teaches you that files fail in several ways — and that the ways prescribe **different, sometimes opposite** actions. Four keys now, each with the instruction it gives a searching role: `unreliable_recall` (a zero hit count proves nothing), `unreliable_order` (**grep is fully reliable and a zero count still counts** — but nothing adjacent to a hit may be read as context or quoted continuously), `not_citable` (leads only; never a page number), `delisted` (**repaired — and read the entry, it may record a limit that survived the repair**). 🔑 The first two are opposites; collapse them into one `degraded` flag and you have told a role to distrust a grep that is, on that file, perfect — and you will never find out, because distrust produces no error. Three rules come with it: **a new key earns its place only if the action it prescribes differs from every existing key's**; 🔴 **a key with no members is not a schema, it is a guess** (one was opened upstream for "sources with no page anchors at all" and retracted the same day — the book that prompted it had 294 of them, under a marker shape the detector had never been written to look for: *the zero was the detector's range, not the book's property*); and **delisting ends the degradation, not the entry**. The self-report contract in `optional-integrations.md` §2 changes with it: **report every category by name, not one count**. Observed upstream — a file moved into the reading-order category and the endpoint's output did not change by one character.
- **`claims-and-evidence` §7 gains a fourth member: the line you added in order to be careful.** A corpus census restricted itself to works of 120 printed pages or more, to stop double-column journal articles inflating a words-per-page statistic — a defensible line. But the condition being hunted *halves a work's page count*: books scanned two printed pages to a sheet. The most affected work in the corpus — 212 printed pages on 106 sheets — fell under the threshold and never entered the census; it surfaced weeks later, by accident. ⇒ **Every filter added to suppress false positives owes the mirror question: is it independent of the thing being detected?** 🔑 *Will this let noise in?* always gets asked. *Will this keep the real cases out?* is the one that decides — and it is asked less **precisely because the filter was written in a spirit of caution, and caution does not feel like a source of error.**
- **Critic — negative universals become a mandatory report section, and one angle stops being skippable for them.** A sentence of the form *no one has / never / the first / the only / the most complete* **can only be falsified, never verified — and no role here has falsifying it as a default task**: discovery searches for support, audit checks citations a draft already made, and an unsourced sweeping negative falls between them. The Critic now lists each one verbatim against **where one would look to overturn it** (it is not asked to read that source), and Angle 3's counterexample and opposing-position questions are compulsory for those sentences whatever the brief emphasises. 🔑 **A selectable rule gets selected away by the brief**: in the case behind this, one critic had passed the same sentence at least **seven times** across revisions, every time under a brief that made a rhetorical angle the obvious choice — it was the role that had looked at that sentence most. The fix is not to weight other rounds more heavily; it is to remove *may skip* for one sentence class.
- **Searcher — the optional field that meant two opposite things, and page anchors.** *Opposing / complicating positions* moves from "always search, report absence" to **mandatory and never blank**: found none ⇒ write `searched, none found` **with the queries and scope you ran**. 🔑 **A negative result is only a result once it carries its denominator** — "I searched these and found nothing" can be overturned; a blank space cannot, because it means *none* and *did not look* at once and the reader cannot tell which. Then a new §1 block: **page markers do not all look alike.** Over 640 extracted files, **466 carried the familiar delimiter form and 174 — 27.2% — carried none of it**, the gap falling hardest on exactly the material that has to be cited by page, being marked instead by a bare number alone on a line, `Page N` alone on a line, or an inline `p. N`. A tool that knows one shape loses a quarter of a corpus **and does not error — it returns "no page number found"**, which reads exactly like a file that has none. Detect each shape with its own pass, never an `if/elif` chain (a mutually-exclusive classifier mis-sorts files carrying two shapes). A two-up file's anchors count *sheets*: **anchors ÷ printed pages ≈ 0.5** is the cheap tell, confirmed by rendering one page and seeing two folios side by side. 🔴 **And a missing anchor is a locating problem, not an evidentiary one** — rendering still works, so a quote is never downgraded on that ground; conflating the two trades a solvable inconvenience for a check that should have happened.
- **The census in §1 gets a scope note it should have had.** v3.5 published a page-alignment census over "the 391 corpus files that carry both a text layer **and page markers**". The probe recognised **one** marker shape. ⇒ **the population was drawn by the detector's blind spot**: the finding stands for what it measured, and never covered the quarter of the corpus its probe could not see. The two counts are from different dates against different denominators and are not to be subtracted.
- **`page-offset-registry` — search the registry before computing an offset.** A worklist reported eleven works missing; **six were already there**, one with a note already recording that the filename's year disagreed with the edition in hand. 🔑 **The duplicated labour is not the point — the direction is**: compute a value that differs from a row you never saw, and you will believe you found an error, and your next act will be to change something that was right.
- **Deep-reader — three pre-delivery checks, and none of them is a new rule.** Search the registry before reporting something missing from it; label every page number by *how you got it* (read off the rendered page, or inferred — a chapter's opening page routinely suppresses the printed folio by design, so "could not read it" is honest and common, while recording an inference as a reading is not); and after filling in a section, go back and fix the count in its heading and check what the credential line claims to cover (one entry headed "33 items · all verified" held 44). 🔑 **All three already existed. They were not being followed — a different problem from not being written — and moving them into the list you must run before delivery does more than writing them a fourth time.**
- 41 files.

## v3.5 — 2026-08-11 · The blind-spot layer

**Every gate has a region it structurally cannot see, and that is where the defect sits.** v3.3 was about a text layer that lies; v3.4 was about rulings that no gate checks. This one is about checks that are healthy, correctly aimed, and still incapable of reporting the thing you need — beginning with a correction to this repo's own spec.

- 🔴 **`claims-and-evidence` §5 is now *five* ways a check dies.** The fifth: **deletion is invisible.** Every gate above it hunts for surplus or difference, and a missing passage produces neither — no stray to sanitize, no mismatch to diff, no syntax to fail, no anchor moved. One lost filename in a changelog line, eaten by a shell command substitution, cleared six gates in a row including a human line-by-line review. 🔑 **The corollary is worth more than the rule: "the two copies match" is a *necessary* condition for completeness, never a sufficient one** — a loss from a single operation lands in both copies, so checksum parity proves the export did not drift and proves nothing about whether the source was whole. *This section had said "four" since v3.2; the family gained its fifth member the same day v3.4 shipped, and the two crossed in the air.*
- **New §7 — what a gate structurally cannot see.** Three of them. **Completeness can only be measured against the source, never the product**: a file that had lost every printed page number, mangled its footnote markup, and misfiled cross-page paragraphs scored *healthy, no action needed* on two independent registers, because both read only the product. 🔑 **What broke thoroughly enough gets caught; what broke just enough to still look right goes straight through.** ⚠ And "against the source" is not yet precise — two probes against the same source disagreed (4-of-7 misaligned vs. 18-of-18 aligned) because one sampled first sentences and the other mid-page; **say which face of the source you measured.** Then: **presence-only gates reward vagueness** (a field reading `not sent` counted as attested), and **the signpost is the thing's most convincing double** (10 of 10 footnote markers verified present; the footnote text they point to had been missing for 34 days).
- **New §8 — the half you did well is why the other half never happens.** Three cases in one day, three people, three objects: a retraction thorough enough to say "that recommendation was mine and was never executed" while two other passages still taught it; a full self-criticism that made its section feel finished, followed by eight more errors of the same shape; a probe validated against 14 negative controls that then could not catch the case which prompted it. ⇒ **The better the finished half reads, the more it needs a last line stating what is still not done.** 📌 Deliberately adds no check — nobody in the three cases was unaware of the second half, so a gate would not have helped.
- **§4 gains a second sibling rule**: *a tool's first-hand source is its code; its output is second-hand.* Diagnosing mechanism from emitted artifacts is inference — and the damage was not the wrong diagnosis but the two fixes prescribed on top of it, both regressions.
- **Searcher §1 — two additions to the page-offset machinery.** A **second, independent cause of the same −1**: layout-aware extractors file a page-spanning paragraph wholly under the page it started on, so a quote from a page block's *opening* has roughly a one-in-five chance of belonging to the page before (census: 391 files / 19,927 pages; mid-page probe aligned 98.0%, first-sentence probe −1 in 18.70% — a 9.4× gap). Rule carries its **exit condition**. And **one thing that is not an offset at all**: online-first articles print a self-contained page range that is not the version of record's — ⚠ the image credential makes this *harder* to doubt, because "verified on the image, printed page 5" is true and wrong.
- **Searcher §2 — numbers inside a table are never taken from an extracted cell.** A version upgrade recovered a **minus sign** the older build had split into a cell of its own (`+0.17` in the product, `−0.17` on the page) — and left the cell alignment wrong in both builds.
- **Librarian §8 — three additions.** 🔑 **The intake probe reads the PDF while the damage happens inside the extractor**: one file measured a healthy 3.42% single-letter rate at source and came out of three extractors at 1.02% / 4.06% / **26.69%** ⇒ choosing an extractor is a decision *followed by a measurement, per file* — and ⚠ *fragment rate* is not *single-letter rate* (43.6% of one work's single letters were legitimate). **Searchability sets severity; provenance decides whether you touch it** — a book's own misprint, corrected, makes every downstream quotation one that does not exist in the source. And a **page-anchor gate**: a two-up split produces a file at twice the original page count, and both numbers report success while counting different objects.

## v3.4 — 2026-07-31 · The ruling layer

**Gates check work; nothing checks a ruling.** This release adds the fourth verification layer, the boundary of the page credential, and a governance section for the class of error no gate had been catching: a decision written down more confidently than its evidence supports.

- **Searcher §2 is now four layers.** New **entity attribution** — is the passage's claim *about* the same subject the draft is about? It is the only layer that **flags rather than discards**, because a near-miss on entity is often the right passage with the wrong framing. With it, **the second cut**: separate what the author asserts from what is *presupposed by the subject the author describes*; an editor's note is not the author's words.
- **claims-and-evidence §1**: the credential's **range**. A ✅ needs three things together — printed-number credential, **verbatim anchor**, and position on the page. *Seeing the page is not verifying the sentence*, and a verification never extrapolates to the next quote. Notation follows: `pp. 9, 11`, never `pp. 9–11`, when the evidence covers two pages and not the span.
- **claims-and-evidence §3**: the **vocabulary rule** — "final state", "no problems", "all clean" are banned; say two falsifiable sentences instead (known classes at zero with a named regression test / N samples read with the sampling method stated). Where you did not measure, write *"not measured — discipline only, no gate"*: **a fabricated sample size is worse than "no problems", because it looks falsifiable.** And a self-declared convergence gets its final confirming round **ordered by the recipient**, not by the reporter — twice here a completion was announced, and twice one more round found real defects.
- **New `claims-and-evidence` §6 — a ruling is wider than its evidence.** Four questions before a ruling ships (how much does it cover / which *version* did it reject / does the evidence mean anything in *this* data / is the criterion sufficient or only necessary), plus the receiving side's duty: **when a report contains two sentences that contradict each other, ask — do not pick one as a premise.**
- **Librarian §8**: three additions — a repair whose wrong output is a **real word** must never be applied in bulk (the safest-looking case is the dangerous one); **intake runs the full ladder by default**, tiering decides how much a human reads, not what runs (file-level quality indicators measured almost no discriminating power; the producer string was the one that earned its place); and **register a defect type the round you name it, even when the ruling is "not fixing this"** — "new" is a judgement relative to a list, so the list's completeness is part of the criterion.
- 41 files.

## v3.3 — 2026-07-20 · The corpus integrity layer

**Your grep can lie.** A file can pass every size check and still be unsearchable — words split apart character by character, a font's private encoding storing `wkh` for `the`, OCR noise that leaves single words findable while every phrase is dead. Three failure families, each invisible to the others' detector. This release ships the counter-machinery.

- **New** `shared/degradation-registry.md` — the honest-degradation pattern: files repaired to their ceiling are **registered, never hidden**; every entry carries which access paths still work; the search index self-reports the degraded count, and an unreadable registry returns *unknown*, never zero.
- **Librarian §8 (new section)**: the three failure families and their detectors; intake risk probes (font-table checks before extraction; the extractor-as-control-group diagnosis rule); three-layer extraction QA (structure / lexicon / **recall** — each catches what the others miss); and the repair method that actually worked — **corpus-statistics-driven repair of the original text** (use the corpus's own word frequencies), where re-extraction reproduced the damage and OCR traded one disease for another.
- **Searcher**: file-level failure judged by the phrase-probe test (single words hit, phrases dead = every negative conclusion about that file is void); a mechanical pre-step before any "not in the vault" ships — consult the registry and the index's `degraded` self-report.
- **optional-integrations**: the self-report contract (living file, read fresh; *unknown-not-zero*; count mismatches surfaced, never silently resolved).
- 41 files.

## v3.1 — 2026-07-16 · The sixth role and the project scaffold

- **New role: Deep-reader** — reads a whole book or lecture series into a structured, page-anchored close-read note; write access bounded to its own notes folder.
